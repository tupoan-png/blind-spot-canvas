TeamTudor · Anna · Miriam · Bogi
Game titleBlind Spot
One-line pitchRate the same six employees — then jump two years ahead and watch your ratings come true. A fast, competitive game that turns appraisal bias into consequences you can see.

1 Player

Who is playing? What motivates them? What do they bring?
  • Players (direct target group): line managers who carry out performance appraisals — plus HR and aspiring managers.
  • Target group of the target group: the employees these managers rate and develop.
  • Motivation: competitive, want to “win”, and curious whether they are actually fair.
  • They bring: real appraisal experience and strong gut opinions — exactly what the game puts to the test.

2 Purpose

Why make this game? What should players learn or experience?
  • Let managers feel their own rating bias — halo, recency, affinity, visibility, commitment bias.
  • Show that fair, transparent instruments (evidence log, rubric, 360°) produce fairer, more accurate ratings.
  • Behaviour goal: shift from gut-rating to evidence-based rating in the real appraisal cycle.

3 Context

Where, when and with whom? What platform or real situation matters?
  • Where/when: a management-training session / the GES closure — live, in person, ~15–20 min.
  • Setup: one laptop + projector, a facilitator, 2–4 small teams playing on one shared screen.
  • Real-world situation: the annual performance-appraisal and rating cycle.

7 Narrative & World

Setting, story, theme, role? How does it support the experience?
  • Setting: “appraisal season” at a fictional firm.
  • Role: you are the line manager reviewing your six direct reports.
  • Characters: recognisable archetypes — the Self-Promoter, the Quiet Workhorse, the Returner, the Recent Stumble, the Old Favourite, the Clear Star.
  • Why it works: putting a face and a story on each bias makes it personal, not abstract.
MDA CORE

4 Mechanics — what the designer builds

Rules • Actions • Goals • Resources • Constraints • Choices
  • Teams rate the same six employees from 1–5; each has a hidden evidence-based “true” rating.
  • Resource: 8 Insight Points per team. Instruments (actions): evidence log ◆1, rating rubric ◆2, 360° feedback ◆3 reveal the truth.
  • Constraint: you can’t afford to investigate everyone — rate on gut or invest in evidence.
  • The twist: the game jumps “two years later” — each rating plays out as a human consequence (thrive / coast / walk).
  • Goal / scoring: accuracy to the fair rating + bias caught + evidence-based − fell for bias. Fairest manager wins.

5 Dynamics — what happens during play

Interactions • Strategies • Competition • Tension • Emergent behaviour
  • Teams argue each rating — gut says one thing, the evidence another.
  • Strategy: spend scarce points on the people you’re least sure about.
  • Players trust their gut, get burned at the reveal, then start seeking evidence.
  • The payoff: seeing your biased ratings cost real people — and your team — two years on.

6 Aesthetics — what the player experiences

Challenge • Discovery • Fellowship • Narrative • Emotion
  • Challenge — beat rivals on fairness. Discovery — reveal the hidden evidence.
  • Fellowship — the team debate. Narrative — the six archetypes.
  • Emotion — the gut-punch of watching your bias make a good person walk out.

8 Feedback

How does the game communicate progress, consequences, success and change?
  • During play: live Insight-Point counter, “x/6 rated” progress, tags appear on reveal (OVER/UNDER-rated, EVIDENCE n/5).
  • Between turns: full-screen “Team B, you’re up” handoffs keep the room oriented.
  • At the end — “two years later”: each rating becomes a human outcome, a thrive / coast / walk tally, and a Bias Report Card naming each team’s blind spot; confetti for the fairest.
  • Change: a debrief prompt turns the result into a Monday action.

9 Technology & Resources

What tools, materials, platform or production resources are needed?
  • Format: a single self-contained web page (HTML/JS) — runs in any browser, no install or login.
  • To run it: a laptop, a projector/screen, a facilitator, 2–4 teams. Sound optional.
  • Backup: the same design also works as a printed card deck.
  • Status: playable prototype already built — only the link is needed.

10 Prototype & Test

Smallest playable version? What will you test, with whom, and what would you observe?
  • Smallest version: the current build with 2 teams and the six employees.
  • Test with: ~4 classmates or line managers in one playthrough.
  • Observe: Do they spend points, or rush on gut? Do they fall for the Self-Promoter? Does the reveal land the “aha”? Does the debrief spark real reflection? Is 15–20 min the right length?

Core Game Experience

Complete the sentence.
“Players will feel the pull of their own appraisal bias and discover that fair, evidence-based ratings change who thrives and who walks, because our game lets them rate real-feeling people, then jumps two years ahead to show the human cost of the bias they missed.”
Design check — trace Player → Mechanic → Dynamic → Aesthetic
Path 1: Line manager (player) → hidden ratings + scarce Insight Points (mechanic) → must choose who to investigate and debate gut vs evidence (dynamic) → Challenge + Discovery (aesthetic).
Path 2: Manager who knows these archetypes (player) → staged reveal comparing their rating to the truth (mechanic) → “I under-rated the quiet one”, discussed as a team (dynamic) → Emotion + Fellowship (aesthetic).
Assumption to test: that managers will actually spend points on evidence instead of rushing on gut — the budget/time balance needs a playtest.
